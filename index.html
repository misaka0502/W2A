W2A: Distilling World Priors into Action Policies for Real-Time Robot Control

Anonymous Authors

W2A is a two-stage distillation framework that transfers world priors from pretrained video models into lightweight action policies for real-time robotic manipulation.

Abstract

World Action Models (WAMs) have recently shown great promise in robotic control, benefiting from the future imagination capability of large-scale pretrained video models, which enables them to capture the physical causality underlying how actions influence the environment. However, most existing WAMs require explicitly imagining future states at inference time, incurring substantial computational overhead and latency that make them unsuitable for real-time robotic control. In this paper, we propose \textbf{W2A}, a two-stage distillation framework for real-time robotic manipulation that distills intermediate denoising representations from a video diffusion model into a lightweight action policy during training, while entirely discarding the video model at inference time. As a result, the policy retains physically grounded world priors while achieving high real-time efficiency. We observe that although video model representations are high-dimensional, only a small subset of task-relevant features is essential for action generation, enabling effective transfer into lightweight policies. Extensive experiments in both simulation and real-world settings demonstrate competitive success rates while significantly improving inference efficiency.

Method

W2A is a two-stage distillation framework that transfers world priors from a video DiT into a lightweight action DiT for real-time robotic manipulation. In Stage I, we adapt a pretrained language-conditioned video DiT to robotic video data and compress its latent representations into a lower-dimensional space, yielding a compact video representation of world priors. In Stage II, we train an action DiT from scratch and augment its standard action modeling objective with a latent distillation objective, which aligns the action latents with the compressed video latents through a lightweight Q-Former bottleneck. At inference time, the video DiT is entirely discarded, and only the action DiT is retained to generate actions conditioned on recent observations and task descriptions.

Results on Simulation and Real-World Tasks

We evaluate W2A on LIBERO and CALVIN simulation benchmarks, as well as on several real-world tasks.

W2A delivers strong results across simulation and real-world robotic manipulation benchmarks. It achieves the best scratch performance on LIBERO, competitive long-horizon performance on CALVIN ABC→D, and consistently outperforms Diffusion Policy and OpenVLA on real-world tasks. These results show that video-based world priors can be effectively distilled into a lightweight action policy without sacrificing performance.

What is actually distilled?

The distilled action latents align more consistently with task-relevant regions in the teacher video latents. This suggests that features from task-relevant and highly dynamic regions are more important for action generation, while task-irrelevant regions such as the background are largely ignored during distillation. Compared with the baseline, whose responses are often scattered and unstable, the distilled model concentrates high responses on the gripper and manipulated objects. Notably, although the probe takes the current-step action latent as input, the highlighted regions also correspond well to task-relevant areas in the future image, suggesting that the distilled representation captures a certain degree of future-aware world priors rather than merely reflecting the current observation.

Inference efficiency

A key goal of W2A is to retain the benefit of video-based world priors without incurring the deployment overhead of an explicit video model at test time. To verify this, we compare inference latency across representative baselines on the same platform. W2A achieves the lowest inference latency among all compared methods, requiring only 68\,ms per action prediction. The efficiency gain comes from the fact that W2A discards the video DiT entirely at inference time and deploys only a lightweight action policy. Moreover, if a lightweight text encoder is used, W2A requires only about 6 GB of GPU memory at deployment, which is a substantial advantage in compute-constrained settings.

Ablation

We compare the full model with two simplified variants: w/o distillation, which removes the distillation loss and trains the action DiT only with the flow-matching objective, and w/o PCA, which directly uses the original teacher latent without feature compression. As shown in Table 1, both variants perform worse than the full model. Removing distillation leads to a clear drop in performance, confirming that the compact teacher video latents provide useful supervision beyond standard action modeling. Meanwhile, directly distilling the uncompressed teacher latent also degrades performance, suggesting that the raw teacher features are overly high-dimensional and noisy for effective transfer. These results show that both the proposed distillation design and PCA-based compression are important for transferring control-relevant world priors into the action policy.
